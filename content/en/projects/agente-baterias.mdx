## Result

- The client was at risk of cancelling, and the solution reversed the churn.
- Customer service now answers known cases on its own and turns unknown ones into catalog entries.
- It opened a new kind of product at the company: an AI agent integrated with a database and a management interface. With that, the AI actually knows the client's business and is far more useful than a generic chatbot.

There was no formal measurement of metrics, so there are no numbers here.

## How it works

The key point is that **the agent and the dashboard close a loop**. Every car the agent doesn't know becomes a task for a salesperson, and every task solved teaches the agent. The knowledge base grows with use.

## Context and problem

A car battery store, with delivery and installation, served its customers on WhatsApp. Finding the right battery means matching the vehicle's **model, year, transmission and start-stop system** against amperage, type (standard or EFB) and case size. That knowledge lived in the salespeople's heads, and every order depended on one of them stopping to work it out.

At the same time, the store was an unhappy Hunt Sales client at risk of cancelling.

## My role

I proposed the project and ran it on my own. I built the system end to end: the agent flow (starting from a standard company flow, shaped for this case), the database model, the management dashboard, login and authentication, and the deployment on the company's domain.

The dashboard runs embedded in Hunt Sales' customer service platform (CRM), so salespeople use everything in one place. It was the first project of its kind at the company; after it, I became responsible for automations and similar systems for other clients.

### Data model

Table names are in Portuguese, as in the code.

- **`baterias`** (batteries): brand, amperage, type (standard or EFB), case size, warranty in months, upfront and installment price, active or paused status.
- **`aplicacoes`** (fitments): vehicle model, year range, transmission, start-stop, recommended amperage and battery type, required case size.
- **`veiculos_sugeridos`** (suggested vehicles): model, year, transmission and start-stop collected by the agent, contact name and a link to the conversation.

- **Battery catalog** grouped by amperage, with search, brand and amperage filters, pagination, and actions to edit, **pause or activate** (takes out-of-stock items offline without deleting them) and delete with confirmation.
- **Vehicle fitments**: the compatibility table, with transmission, recommended amperage, case size and a start-stop highlight.
- **IA Sugere** (AI suggests): the queue of cars the agent couldn't find during conversations. Each card has “Ver chat”, which opens the original conversation, and “+ Cadastrar” (add).
- **Prefilled form**: it opens with model, year, transmission and start-stop from the AI. The salesperson only completes the battery spec and saves; the suggestion is resolved automatically.
- **Login** with Supabase Auth (email and password), a session limited to the browser tab and every page protected.

## Stack

- **Agent:** GPT-4o mini (OpenAI), on Hunt Sales' customer service platform, over WhatsApp.
- **Database and authentication:** Supabase (PostgreSQL and Auth, with RLS).
- **Dashboard:** HTML and JavaScript with Tailwind CSS, static, no server of its own. Deployed on the company's domain and embedded in the CRM.

## Decisions and trade-offs

<Decision
  title="A human in the loop for anything uncertain"
  tradeoff="For unknown cars, the customer waits for the team instead of getting an instant answer."
>
  The agent never guesses a battery for an unknown car: it hands over to the team and creates a pending task in the dashboard. The wrong battery means a lost delivery and a frustrated customer.
</Decision>

<Decision
  title="The AI collects, the human confirms"
  tradeoff="Every new car still needs someone who knows the product to complete the entry."
>
  The technical part (amperage, type and case) stays with whoever knows the product. The AI saves the typing for the rest: the form arrives filled in with what was said in the conversation.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Questions in the right order">
  First, the agent asks whether the customer already knows the amperage. If not, it collects model, year, transmission and start-stop, which are exactly the keys of the fitment table. Each answer becomes a filter in the query.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Pause instead of delete">
  Every battery has an active or paused status, and the agent only offers active ones. That way the store takes out-of-stock items offline without losing the entry.
</Decision>

<Decision
  title="A simple, static front-end"
  tradeoff="With no server of its own, data protection relies on authentication and Supabase's access rules."
>
  HTML and JavaScript on top of Supabase, with no server of its own, so it stays light and easy to deploy on the company's domain.
</Decision>

## The same pattern in another niche

The same pattern was replicated for an electric vehicle charger company: see the [dashboard for electric vehicle chargers](/en/projects/carregadores-ev).

## What I learned

- In applied AI, the value is as much in the system around the model (data, dashboard, handoff) as in the prompt.
- Design for "I don't know" first: the unknown-vehicle path is what made the system reliable.
- The "agent + database + dashboard for humans" pattern is reusable: it worked for batteries and then for EV chargers.
